FAQ
Why use this?
You get to see real, live sensor data straight from actual weather balloons in flight right now — the same raw measurements meteorologists use to calibrate forecasts. It's a genuine look at the atmosphere, not a prediction.
Where does this data come from?
Live radiosonde (weather balloon) telemetry from sonde.mine.nu (its sondes.php feed) from SondeHub (sondehub.org, an open worldwide database of radiosonde telemetry) and from NOAA's Integrated Global Radiosonde Archive (IGRA), the official soundings of about 650 weather-service stations in more than 100 countries — real sensor readings from balloons currently or recently in flight, not a forecast model.
Why are some temperatures missing from the list?
Radiosondes spend most of a flight at high altitude, where it's routinely −40°C to −70°C. Those readings aren't representative of ground weather, so this app filters them out and only keeps readings at or above 0°C — typically captured near launch or landing.
Why does it say a station is far from me?
This isn't a dense ground-sensor network — it's wherever balloons happened to fly recently. If nothing launched near you in the last 7 days, the nearest valid reading may be hundreds of km away. The app always labels the distance honestly instead of pretending it's local.
Why does location sometimes look approximate?
Precise GPS location requires a secure (HTTPS) connection. If you're on a plain http:// address, the app falls back to IP-based location, which is only accurate to city level.
How often does data update?
The server refetches the last 7 days of readings automatically on a schedule; the page itself also polls for new data every 5 minutes.
What do Humidity/Pressure show "—"?
Not every sonde model carries a working humidity or pressure sensor — some report temperature only. A dash means that particular sensor had no data, not an app error.

Live weather from radiosonde balloons

Groundcheck shows the temperature, air pressure and humidity measured at ground level by weather balloons (radiosondes) around the world, as a map and as a weather app. Radiosondes are launched from stations every day, and the first reading each one sends from the ground is a real measurement taken at that spot.

Where the data comes from: the live radiosonde feed of sonde.mine.nu, amateur balloon tracking from SondeHub, and the NOAA IGRA archive of official weather-service launches. Readings come from several hundred stations across Europe, North and South America, Asia, Africa, Australia and the Pacific islands.

The forecast: Groundcheck's own forecast for the next hours and days is built only from these balloon readings (seasonal normals, how today differs from normal, and the pressure trend). It does not use a weather model and it can be wrong; the accuracy page shows how the big weather models scored against what balloons measured.

Free to use: the web app, the Firefox add-on, a public data API, and a self-host package to run your own copy. We also make Flowsearch, a search engine with its own crawler and index. See also the 3D globe of every reading.
