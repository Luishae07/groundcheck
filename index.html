FAQ
Why use this?
You get to see real, live sensor data straight from actual weather balloons in flight right now — the same raw measurements meteorologists use to calibrate forecasts. It's a genuine look at the atmosphere, not a prediction.
Where does this data come from?
Live radiosonde (weather balloon) telemetry from sonde.mine.nu, specifically its sondes.php feed — real sensor readings from balloons currently or recently in flight, not a forecast model.
Why are some temperatures missing from the list?
Radiosondes spend most of a flight at high altitude, where it's routinely −40°C to −70°C. Those readings aren't representative of ground weather, so this app filters them out and only keeps readings at or above 0°C — typically captured near launch or landing.
Why does it say a station is far from me?
This isn't a dense ground-sensor network — it's wherever balloons happened to fly recently. If nothing launched near you in the last 7 days, the nearest valid reading may be hundreds of km away. The app always labels the distance honestly instead of pretending it's local.
Why does location sometimes look approximate?
Precise GPS location requires a secure (HTTPS) connection. If you're on a plain http:// address, the app falls back to IP-based location, which is only accurate to city level.
How often does data update?
The server refetches the last 7 days of readings automatically on a schedule; the page itself also polls for new data every 5 minutes.
What do Humidity/Pressure show "—"?
Not every sonde model carries a working humidity or pressure sensor — some report temperature only. A dash means that particular sensor had no data, not an app error.
